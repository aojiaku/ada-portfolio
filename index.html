The Problem Library

Every day, frontline employees were spotting real problems: bugs, gaps, patterns worth flagging. But that feedback was scattered across emails, chats, and forms, with no way to tell a problem that kept coming up from a one-time complaint. This tool turns over 1,600 individual reports into a clear, organized list that leaders can actually act on. You can also ask it questions directly, instead of digging through a spreadsheet.

Click any row below to see what someone actually said.

Ask About These Problems

This isn't a live AI chatbot. It's a simple tool that reads your question and finds the answer in the data above. It works a lot like how I actually use AI at work: AI helps me find patterns fast, but a person still decides what actually counts as a problem.

Queue Health Monitor

Leaders had plenty of data, but no single place to see how any team was actually doing. Case volume, on-time rates, quality scores, and speed all lived in different tools, so a 70-slide weekly meeting could explain what happened but rarely why. This puts every team on the same three simple measures: how much work is coming in, how fast it's handled, and how well it's done.

Click a team card to see its 8-week trend and what's actually going on behind it.

Capacity Model

My team juggled four different kinds of work at the same time: regular reporting meetings, big strategic projects, everyday casework, and last-minute fires to put out. I could explain any single project, but not our total workload or where the real tradeoffs were. This tool connects team size and workload to what's actually needed in each area, so a conversation about priorities can start with real numbers instead of a gut feeling.

Drag any slider below and watch the numbers update.

Quick note: the category names and hours below are made up. The idea behind it, team size and hours compared to what each area actually needs, matches a real planning tool I built and use.

How this site was built

Human + AI collaboration. Real artifacts. Deployed by me.

This site started with ChatGPT: I used it to draft the original career narrative and get an early version live on Vercel. Since then, I've worked with Claude to complete and refine it, both used to make direct edits to the source code, not just drafting. I gave them 20+ years of performance reviews, career data, and project history, and we iterated together on the structure, the copy, and the six interactive tools.

Then I took it from prototype to production myself: wiring it to GitHub for version control, and deploying it on Vercel.
